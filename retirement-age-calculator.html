The 4% rule explained

The most common rule of thumb: withdraw 4% of your savings in the first year of retirement, then adjust for inflation. This gives you about a 95% chance of your money lasting 30 years.

That means you need roughly 25× your annual spending saved up before you can retire. Want $60,000 a year? You need $1.5 million.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Underestimating healthcare costs

Healthcare is the biggest wild card in retirement. Fidelity estimates a 65-year-old couple needs $300,000+ in healthcare costs alone. Plan for it.

Planning for 20 years, living 30

If you retire at 62 and live to 90, that's 28 years. The 4% rule is designed for a 30-year retirement horizon — don't cut it close.
