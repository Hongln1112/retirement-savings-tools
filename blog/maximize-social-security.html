How to Maximize Your Social Security Benefits

Updated October 2026 · 7 min read

Social Security is the foundation of most Americans' retirement income. Yet the claiming decision — when to start taking it — is one of the most consequential financial choices you'll ever make. Get it wrong, and you could lose tens of thousands of dollars over your lifetime.

The Claiming Ages

62 (earliest)Reduced by ~30%70%
Full Retirement Age (67 for those born 1960+)Full benefit100%
70 (delayed)+8% per year of delay after FRA124%

Your monthly check grows by about 8% for every year you delay claiming past your Full Retirement Age (FRA), up until age 70. After 70, there's no further increase — no reason to wait longer.

The Break-Even Age

If you claim at 62 instead of 67, you get 5 years of smaller checks. At what age do the larger delayed checks overtake the early ones?

The break-even age is roughly 82–84. If you live past your mid-80s (which, with modern medicine, is increasingly likely), waiting until 70 pays off. If you have serious health issues that suggest a shorter lifespan, claiming earlier makes sense.

Spousal Benefits

If you're married, you have more strategies than just "when do I claim?" A spouse can claim up to 50% of the higher earner's full benefit, even if they never worked. When the higher earner passes away, the surviving spouse receives the higher benefit for life.

This means the higher earner should almost always delay to age 70 — not just for their own benefit, but to maximize the survivor benefit that lasts for two lifetimes.

Three Common Strategies

1. The Higher Earner Delays, The Lower Earner Claims Early. This locks in spousal income while letting the larger benefit grow. The higher earner waits until 70.

2. Both Delay to 70. If you have enough other savings to cover retirement spending until 70, this maximizes the guaranteed lifetime income stream. Best for healthy couples with longevity in the family.

3. Claim at 62, Keep Working. If you keep working while claiming before FRA, your benefits are temporarily reduced ($1 for every $2 earned over ~$22,000/year in 2026). After FRA, there's no earnings limit. This strategy only makes sense if you need the cash.

Working After Claiming

If you claim before your Full Retirement Age and continue working, the Social Security earnings test withholds some of your benefit. But don't worry — it's not lost. When you reach FRA, Social Security recalculates your benefit to give you credit for the withheld amounts.

Don't Forget Your Earnings Record

Your benefit is based on your 35 highest-earning years. Check your record at ssa.gov/myaccount annually to make sure all your employers have reported correctly. A missing year can reduce your benefit permanently.
