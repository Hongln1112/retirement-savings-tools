Under the Rule of 75, you qualify for full retirement benefits the moment your age plus your total years of service add up to 75 — for example, age 50 with 25 years of service, or age 55 with 20 years. It's a common threshold in roles where people start working later in life, like law enforcement or certain union jobs. How the Rule of 75 works

The Rule of 75 says your age plus your years of service must total 75 before you can retire with full pension or retirement benefits. It's commonly found in plans where employees tend to start later, so the lower threshold gives them a realistic path to full benefits.

Every year you keep working, both your age and your service count climb by one — so your combined total rises by two each year. That means reaching 75 isn't about waiting until a fixed age; it's about the interaction between how long you've worked and how old you are.

For example: you're 48 with 20 years of service (total 68). You're 7 short of 75, and since your total grows by 2 per year, you'll qualify in 4 more years — at age 52 with 24 years of service.

Rule of 75 vs. Rule of 80

If your plan uses a higher threshold, you'll need more combined age and service. The Rule of 80 (age + service = 80) qualifies you for full benefits later than the Rule of 75. Check your plan documents to confirm which rule applies. If yours uses 80, use our Rule of 80 calculator.
