Why inflation matters for retirement

If you're 35 and planning to retire at 65, that's 30 years of inflation. At 3% annually, prices roughly double every 24 years. That means a $60,000/year lifestyle today will cost about $120,000/year in 25 years.

This is why keeping all your savings in cash is dangerous — it's slowly being eaten away. Investing in stocks and real estate is the best hedge against inflation.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Thinking cash is safe

Cash in a savings account at 0.5% interest while inflation is 3% loses 2.5% of purchasing power per year. Over 20 years, that's nearly 40% of your wealth.

Ignoring healthcare inflation

Healthcare costs have historically risen 5–6% per year, well above general inflation. If you're retiring early, this hits hardest.

FAQ

Too much money chasing too few goods. It can come from government stimulus, supply chain issues, rising wages, or rising energy prices. The Federal Reserve targets about 2% inflation per year.

Stocks historically beat inflation by about 7% per year after inflation. Real estate, commodities, and TIPS (Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities) are also common hedges. Cash bonds usually barely keep up.

Moderate inflation (2–3%) is actually healthy — it encourages spending and investment. Deflation (falling prices) is much worse, because people stop spending and the economy stalls.
