How Much Should I Have Saved for Retirement by Age?

The most common retirement question: "Am I on track?" Here are simple benchmarks based on your income and age.

Financial planners often use a simple rule of thumb: by age 30, you should have saved 1× your annual salary. By age 40, 3×. By age 50, 6×. By age 60, 8×. By retirement (age 67), 10×.

These numbers come from Fidelity's retirement savings guidelines, which are based on typical spending patterns and the 4% withdrawal rule. They're benchmarks, not strict targets — everyone's situation is different.

What If I'm Behind?

Don't panic. Most Americans are behind on retirement savings. The Federal Reserve's 2023 Survey of Consumer Finances found that the median retirement account balance for households aged 35–44 is only around $60,000 — far below the 2× benchmark.

If you're behind, you have three levers: save more, work longer, or increase your investment returns. The easiest to control is saving more. Even an extra 1% of your salary (about $750/year on $75k) makes a huge difference over 30 years due to compound interest.

How Much Do I Need to Save Monthly?

Using the 4% withdrawal rule, you need 25× your annual spending saved. If you want $60,000/year in retirement, you need $1.5 million.

Starting at age 35 with $0 saved, earning 7% average returns, you'd need to save about $1,100 per month to reach $1.5 million by age 65. That's roughly 18% of a $75,000 salary.
